Hi {{first_name | default: "there"}},

At the start of the school year, behavior referrals often arrive as labels instead of decision questions: *defiant*, *aggressive*, or *needs an FBA*.

Before I choose an assessment path, I want the team to answer three questions:

1. **What did the student do that someone could observe and count?** Replace the label with a description of the behavior, setting, and routine.
2. **Is there an immediate safety, disciplinary, or procedural action the team must address?** Follow your district's requirements before treating the referral as routine consultation.
3. **What decision does the team need the assessment to support?** The next step may be consultation, record review, direct observation, additional data, or a formal assessment process.

Then assign an owner and a review date. A referral without either can remain urgent without becoming clearer.

Try this this week:

1. Pull one new or unresolved referral.
2. Rewrite the concern in observable terms.
3. Add one sentence that names the decision the team needs to make next.

The School BCBA Transformation Program includes applied work on intake, referral routing, assessment decisions, and caseload systems.
